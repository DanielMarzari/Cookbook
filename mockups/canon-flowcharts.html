Eight flowcharts for one dish

Ice cream in every one, because its exits are dishes people already order by name: leave the dairy out and it is sorbet, skip the churn and it is semifreddo, pull the fat and air down and it is gelato. Same three conditions and same three exits throughout — what changes is the shape of the thinking.

01

Decision ladder

Straight down the middle, one condition at a time. Failing a condition doesn’t end the chart — it exits sideways into the dish you actually made.

02

Branching tree

Reads as descent: every dish here is the same base with one decision taken differently. The neighbours are cousins, not failures.

03

Track and sidings

Horizontal, so it fits above a recipe rather than beside it. Stations are the conditions; the sidings are where you end up if you get off early.

04

Funnel

Starts at everything frozen and sweet and narrows. What each stage strains out is named on the way past — the width does the arguing.

05

Nested sets

Not a flow but a containment: each condition is a boundary, and the neighbours sit in the ring where they stopped. Shows at a glance that gelato is closer in than sorbet.

06

Hub and spokes

The dish in the middle, held up by three conditions. Pull any spoke and you fall to the dish named just beyond it. No implied order, which is honest — these don’t really happen in sequence.

07

Forks in a path

A journey with three chances to turn off. The forks rise above the line so the main path stays the strongest thing on the page.

08

Territory

Two dials that actually vary — fat and air — with the region that counts as ice cream boxed on it. The only one that shows how close a neighbour is, and where your own version sits.
