Side-by-side compare

A branched recipe is not a parent with children — it is one dish held in two or three hands at once. Variations sit as parallel columns on a single shared spine, so an ingredient row reads straight across. Rows every branch agrees on go quiet; the cells that actually diverge take the ink, and the hairline gutter between columns darkens exactly where they do.

How the branching reads

The photographs are the first row of the table, not a hero — each column is headed by its own frame, so the eye learns the column order before it ever reaches an ingredient. Everything below inherits that order: at-a-glance figures, ingredient rows, and the one method step where the branches part company.

Emphasis is carried by two things only, both already in the system: ink colour (#111111 against #767676) and the hairline gutter. Where a cell is unlike its neighbours the gutter to its left turns black, so scanning down the columns you see the rule break into dark ticks precisely at the differences. Nothing is tinted, badged or boxed.

The collage

The 4:5 frame is cut into five vertical slices on hairline gutters — the base takes a wider slice at the left, the four branches follow in the order they were added. It is the compare table seen end-on, so the tile and the page teach the same reading direction.

The indicator

Three things mark it, none of them a coloured badge: the sliced composition itself, the four-bar column glyph in a white hairline chip at the foot of the frame, and the tag line reading "other · 4 variations" where a single recipe would read only its cuisine.

Try it

Hover the tile and the slice captions come up; hover one slice and it takes the ink. Click any slice to load that branch as a column in the compare table above — the tile is a column picker in miniature.
