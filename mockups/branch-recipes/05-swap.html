Inline swap chips

The recipe stays one document. The variable part of the ingredient list is a row of selectable chips set inline in the running text — toss with [cinnamon-maple] [lavender + coconut]. Picking one rewrites that line, the matching method step, the nutrition read-out and the hero photo in place. No tabs, no sub-pages, no second column: the branch is a sentence you finish yourself.

    How the branch reads here

    1. 01The tag line says american · 4 variations — the only added furniture above the fold.
    2. 02The chips sit inside two sentences (the finish — toss with, and step 04). They are read as words, not as a control panel.
    3. 03One click rewrites four things at once: the three variant ingredient rows, the tail of step 04, the nutrition figures and the hero photo. Rewritten regions flash #F4F4F4 for a beat so the change is traceable.
    4. 04Both chip rows are the same control. Selecting in the method column moves the ingredient column too, and the reverse.
    5. 05Cost of the feature on this page: one italic sub-header and one extra line of type. Everything else is the recipe that was already there.

    The collage and the branch indicator

    1. 01Composition: one dominant panel plus a vertical strip of three slivers, separated by 2px white gutters. It reads one recipe with three alternates, not a four-up contact sheet — which is exactly what the swap-chip model is.
    2. 02Indicator one: the white hairline gutters. A single-photo tile is edge to edge; a branched tile is visibly cut.
    3. 03Indicator two: the tag line reads american · 4 variations where an ordinary tile reads italian.
    4. 04Indicator three: a row of miniature chips under the meta — the same control as the recipe page, at 9px. Click one and the dominant panel becomes that finish; the other three fall back into the strip. The grid teaches the recipe page.
    5. 05Shared state: the two sections here are wired together on purpose. Pick a finish on the recipe page and the home tile leads with it — the tile shows the version you last cooked, not an arbitrary default.

    Trade-offs

    concept 05

    Gains: the lightest possible footprint — no new routes, no tabs, no accordion, no second scroll. A variation is a data row, not a document. Notes, photos, the cook log and the flavour card stay singular, so a family with four finishes still has one page of history rather than four thin ones.

    Costs: a variation cannot diverge far. If Lavender-Coconut ever needs its own bake time or a sixth step, this model breaks and the recipe has to be forked properly. There is also no permalink for a single finish — you can send someone Spiced Nuts, not Spiced Nuts, chili-lime, unless a query parameter is added later.

    Best for: finishes, glazes, spice blends, fillings, sauces — the last-toss family. Wrong for variations that change the method.
