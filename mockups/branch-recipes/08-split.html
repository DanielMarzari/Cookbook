Cookbook · branched recipes · concept 08

Split master-detail

A branched recipe opens as two panes. A narrow rail on the left holds the base and every variation as a thumbnail index; the right pane is the ordinary recipe page, re-rendered for whatever the rail has selected. Nothing is hidden behind a menu, so a recipe with eight branches reads exactly like a recipe with two. On the home grid the same split becomes the tile: an index strip of five, plus the version you are looking at.

The reading column keeps its canonical measure; the rail hangs in the left margin and the page container widens from max-w-4xl to max-w-6xl only when a recipe has branches. The rail is sticky, so the index stays put while the method scrolls. A black hairline in the left gutter of an ingredient or step marks a line that differs from the base — the only diff signal the concept needs.

A — mobile fallback the rail becomes a screen, not a drawer

Under 900px the two panes stop being side by side and become two screens with a push transition: the rail becomes a full-width index, tapping a row opens the detail, and the detail keeps a back link to the index. So you never have to return to the index for the common case, the detail carries a sibling stepper pinned above the tab bar — previous / next moves between versions in place. Tap the rows on the left frame, or the arrows on the right one.

How the indicator is carried, in order of loudness. 1 — the split itself: five frames where every other tile has one, readable at thumbnail size and in a 1-column phone grid. 2 — the base + 4 stamp, the same white/hairline chip already used for the card hover controls, so it costs no new vocabulary. 3 — 5 versions in the tag line, which is also what search and filters count. The title stays Spiced Nuts: one recipe, one link, one place in the alphabet.

where it wins, where it costs

Wins: scales to eight or eighteen branches without redesign; the detail pane is the unmodified recipe page, so nothing in the reading experience is rebuilt; switching is one click with no scroll loss, and the rail doubles as a table of contents. Costs: it spends 236px of horizontal room permanently and pushes the page container past its 4xl reading width; on mobile it turns into two screens, which is one more tap than an inline swap; and a recipe with a single variation looks over-engineered — below two branches the rail should collapse to a plain line under the title.
