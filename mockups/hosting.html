Mockup · Hosting

Having people over

The menu is the part that gets designed. Everything that makes the night go is somewhere else — in your head, on a torn envelope, or forgotten until someone asks for a fork you don't have. Seven of those, made into something.

02

One page, counting down to the doorbell, built backwards from when you want to sit. Printable, and the only document you look at on the day — the recipes have already been read by then.

06

The invitation, which asks the right question

Sent in the menu's own aesthetic, and carrying the one question that otherwise gets asked at the table. The replies land straight in the diet matrix, so the menu is checked against real answers rather than your memory of them.

Catches: the gap between what people tell you and what they tell a form. Tapping a chip is easier than composing a sentence about your own diet, so you find out about the tree nuts on Tuesday instead of at the table.

Other things in this direction, not drawn

  • Who sits where Seating is the one hosting decision with no data behind it and real consequences. Worth a sketch of the table you can drag names onto — and it would remember last time, so the same two people aren't paired every dinner.
  • Hand this to a helper The run sheet, filtered to the jobs someone else can do without asking you a question. Printed, it is the difference between help and company.
  • Scale with a spine Eight people is not four people doubled — a braise scales cleanly, a custard doesn't, a fried thing gets worse. Flag which recipes lie when multiplied.
  • Leftovers, planned What Sunday looks like, decided on Thursday. Buying the bigger shin because it makes a second meal is a different shop.
  • The same night again A menu you've cooked, saved whole — courses, run sheet, shop and all — so the second time is an evening rather than a project.
  • Music as long as dinner Slight, but the run sheet already knows you sit at 19:40 and reach sweet around 21:55, which is a length.
